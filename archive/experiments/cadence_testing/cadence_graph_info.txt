Inference 1 (The Aliasing Cliff): The pipeline can mathematically survive a seasonal gap of up to ~40 days. Looking at the top-left graph, the period error stays near zero until $x \approx 40$. At 50 days, the error jumps off a cliff, increasing by 3 orders of magnitude (log scale) as Lomb-Scargle locks onto a false window-function alias.
Inference 2 (The Power Bleed): The moon won't completely destroy our period detection, but it drains confidence. The top-right graph shows a clean, linear decay in Lomb-Scargle power as you delete more days per month, dropping from a power of ~26 down to ~21.
Inference 3 (The 20% Phase Limit): Our 3-variable linear algebra solver breaks if it misses more than 20% of the periastron passage. In the bottom-left graph, the fitted amplitude ($K$) tracks the true 45 m/s perfectly until the gap fraction hits 0.20. After that, the linearizer completely loses its anchor, wildly guessing amplitudes anywhere from 0 to over 300 m/s.
Inference 4 (Eccentricity Chaos): Just like the amplitude, the eccentricity (bottom-right) holds steady at 0.6 until the 0.20 phase gap. Once the sharp velocity peak is deleted, the optimizer no longer has the geometric data required to constrain the shape of the orbit, causing it to bounce randomly between circular ($e \approx 0$) and the hard upper boundary ($e = 0.95$).
